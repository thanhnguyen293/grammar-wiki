# some / any · much / many / a lot of

We use these words to say **a little, a few, a lot**, or **none at all**. Which word you choose depends on two things: whether the noun is **countable or uncountable**, and whether the sentence is **positive, negative or a question**.

## Formula

::: info FORMULA
| Word | Goes with | Common sentence type | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| some | plural countable + uncountable | positive, offers, requests | I bought **some** eggs and **some** milk. |
| any | plural countable + uncountable | negative, questions | We don't have **any** eggs or **any** milk. |
| many | plural countable | negative, questions | Do you have **many** friends here? |
| much | uncountable | negative, questions | I don't have **much** time. |
| a lot of / lots of | plural countable + uncountable | all types, especially positive | She has **a lot of** books and **a lot of** free time. |
:::

### Table by sentence type

| Sentence type | Plural countable nouns | Uncountable nouns |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | I have **some** / **a lot of** friends. | I have **some** / **a lot of** money. |
| Negative | I don't have **any** / **many** friends. | I don't have **any** / **much** money. |
| Question | Do you have **any** / **many** friends? | Do you have **any** / **much** money? |
| Offer, request | Would you like **some** cookies? | Can I have **some** water, please? |

::: warning IMPORTANT
**many** only goes with **countable** nouns (*many books, many people*). **much** only goes with **uncountable** nouns (*much water, much time*). If you're not sure, use **a lot of**, which goes with both.
:::

## How to use

### 1. some in positive sentences: a little, a few

- I need **some** help with my homework.<br>*Tôi cần giúp một chút với bài tập.*
- There are **some** apples on the table.<br>*Có vài quả táo trên bàn.*
- She bought **some** new clothes for Tet.<br>*Cô ấy mua vài bộ quần áo mới để đón Tết.*
- We had **some** rain last night.<br>*Tối qua có mưa một chút.*

### 2. any in negatives and questions: any at all

- I don't have **any** money.<br>*Tôi không có đồng nào.*
- There aren't **any** buses after 10 p.m.<br>*Sau 10 giờ tối không còn chuyến xe buýt nào.*
- Do you have **any** brothers or sisters?<br>*Bạn có anh chị em nào không?*
- Is there **any** milk in the fridge?<br>*Trong tủ lạnh còn sữa không?*

### 3. some in offers and requests

Even in a question, when you **offer** or **ask for** something (and expect the answer "yes"), use **some**.

- Would you like **some** tea?<br>*Bạn uống chút trà nhé?*
- Can I have **some** bread, please?<br>*Cho tôi xin ít bánh mì được không?*
- Could you lend me **some** money?<br>*Bạn cho tôi vay ít tiền được không?*
- Do you want **some** help with those bags?<br>*Bạn có cần giúp xách mấy cái túi đó không?*

### 4. any in positive sentences: any one, it doesn't matter which

When *any* is in a positive sentence, it means **it doesn't matter which**.

- You can sit at **any** table.<br>*Bạn có thể ngồi bàn nào cũng được.*
- Call me **any** time.<br>*Gọi cho tôi bất kỳ lúc nào.*
- **Any** child can learn to swim.<br>*Đứa trẻ nào cũng có thể học bơi.*
- Choose **any** colour you like.<br>*Chọn bất kỳ màu nào bạn thích.*

### 5. many and much: a lot

Used mostly in **negatives** and **questions**. To ask about amounts: **How many** + countable noun, **How much** + uncountable noun (and prices).

- There aren't **many** people here today.<br>*Hôm nay ở đây không đông người lắm.*
- I didn't sleep **much** last night.<br>*Tối qua tôi ngủ không được nhiều.*
- **How many** children do you have?<br>*Anh chị có mấy con?*
- **How much** sugar do you want in your coffee?<br>*Bạn muốn cho bao nhiêu đường vào cà phê?*

### 6. a lot of / lots of: a lot

Works with **both kinds** of nouns, in **all sentence types**, but is most common in positive sentences. *lots of* is more informal.

- My grandma has **a lot of** stories about the old days.<br>*Bà tôi có rất nhiều chuyện về ngày xưa.*
- He drinks **a lot of** water every day.<br>*Anh ấy uống rất nhiều nước mỗi ngày.*
- There were **lots of** people at the market.<br>*Ở chợ có rất đông người.*
- Do you spend **a lot of** money on clothes?<br>*Bạn có tiêu nhiều tiền vào quần áo không?*

::: tip TIP
In positive sentences, *much* sounds very formal: *I have **much** time* sounds strange. Say *I have **a lot of** time* instead. When there is no noun after it, use **a lot** (without *of*): *I like it **a lot**. Thanks **a lot**.*
:::

## The something / anything / nothing / everything family

*some, any, no, every* combine with *-one/-body* (people), *-thing* (things), *-where* (places). The rules are the same as for *some* and *any* above.

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| someone / somebody | a person (we don't say who) | **Someone** is knocking at the door. |
| anyone / anybody | a person (negatives, questions) / any person at all | Is **anybody** home? |
| no one / nobody | no person | **Nobody** answered the phone. |
| everyone / everybody | all people | **Everyone** loves her cooking. |
| something | a thing (we don't say which) | I want to eat **something** sweet. |
| anything | a thing (negatives, questions) / any thing at all | Do you need **anything** from the shop? |
| nothing | no thing | There's **nothing** in the box. |
| everything | all things | **Everything** is ready. |
| somewhere | a place (we don't say where) | I left my glasses **somewhere**. |
| anywhere | a place (negatives, questions) / any place at all | I can't find my keys **anywhere**. |
| nowhere | no place | There's **nowhere** to park. |
| everywhere | all places | I looked **everywhere** for you. |

- **Somebody** called you this morning.<br>*Sáng nay có ai đó gọi cho bạn.*
- I didn't buy **anything** at the market.<br>*Tôi không mua gì ở chợ cả.*
- Would you like **something** to drink?<br>*Bạn muốn uống gì không?* (an offer → *something*)
- You can go **anywhere** you like.<br>*Bạn có thể đi bất cứ đâu bạn thích.*

### Two things to remember

**1. The verb is singular.** Even though they mean "all people", *everyone, somebody, nobody…* take a **singular** verb.

- **Everybody is** here.<br>*Mọi người đều có mặt.*
- **Nothing was** wrong.<br>*Không có gì sai cả.*
- **Somebody has** taken my pen.<br>*Ai đó đã lấy bút của tôi.*

**2. No double negatives.** *nobody, nothing, nowhere* already mean "not", so the verb stays **positive**.

- I saw **nobody**. = I **didn't** see **anybody**.<br>*Tôi không thấy ai cả.*
- She said **nothing**. = She **didn't** say **anything**.<br>*Cô ấy không nói gì cả.*
- **Nobody** knows the answer.<br>*Không ai biết câu trả lời.*

## Common mistakes

::: danger COMMON MISTAKES
| ✗ Wrong | ✓ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I don't have some money. | I don't have **any** money. | Negatives use *any*. |
| How much people came? | How **many** people came? | *people* is countable, so use *many*. |
| I have much friends. | I have **a lot of** friends. | *friends* is countable; in a positive sentence, use *a lot of*. |
| I didn't see nobody. | I didn't see **anybody**. / I saw **nobody**. | Don't use two negative words in one sentence. |
| Everybody are happy. | Everybody **is** happy. | *everybody* takes a singular verb. |
:::

## In context

> **Linh:** Let's make phở tonight. Do we have **any** beef? \
> **Quân:** There's **some** beef in the fridge, but not **much**. \
> **Linh:** And noodles? \
> **Quân:** We don't have **any** noodles. And we don't have **many** onions, either. \
> **Linh:** OK, I'll go to the market. Do you need **anything** else? \
> **Quân:** Yes, can you buy **some** herbs? And get **a lot of** limes. **Everybody** loves lime in their phở!

::: details Vietnamese translation
**Linh:** Tối nay mình nấu phở đi. Nhà mình còn thịt bò không?<br>
**Quân:** Trong tủ lạnh còn ít thịt bò, nhưng không nhiều.<br>
**Linh:** Còn bánh phở?<br>
**Quân:** Hết bánh phở rồi. Hành cũng không còn nhiều.<br>
**Linh:** Được, em sẽ đi chợ. Anh cần gì nữa không?<br>
**Quân:** Có, em mua ít rau thơm nhé. Mua nhiều chanh vào. Ai cũng thích vắt chanh vào phở!
:::

## Remember

::: warning SUMMARY
- **some**: positive sentences, offers, requests. **any**: negatives, questions; in a positive sentence it means **it doesn't matter which**.
- **many** + countable, **much** + uncountable, mostly in negatives and questions.
- **a lot of** goes with both kinds and works in all sentence types.
- *everyone, nobody, something…* + **singular** verb; *nobody, nothing* don't go with *not*.
:::
