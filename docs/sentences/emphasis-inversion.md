# Emphasis and inversion

Use these structures to **highlight** one part of a sentence ("It was **Nam** who broke the glass") or to make a sentence sound **stronger and more formal** ("**Never** have I seen…"). You often meet them in speeches, in writing, and in sentence-rewriting exercises.

## Formula

::: info FORMULA
| Form | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Cleft sentence | **It is / It was** + the emphasised part + **that / who** + the rest | **It was** Nam **who** broke the glass.<br>*Chính Nam là người làm vỡ cái cốc.* |
| Emphasising the verb | S + **do / does / did** + V | I **do** love this song.<br>*Tôi thật sự rất thích bài hát này.* |
| Inversion | Negative / limiting word + **auxiliary** + S + V | **Never have I** seen such a big fish.<br>*Chưa bao giờ tôi thấy con cá nào to như vậy.* |
:::

## Cleft sentences: It is / It was … that / who

Put the part you want to emphasise between **It is / It was** and **that / who**. Use **who** (or *that*) for people and **that** for everything else. Use **is** for the present and **was** for the past.

From one original sentence, you can emphasise different parts:

- Original sentence: Nam met Lan at the café yesterday.<br>*Hôm qua Nam gặp Lan ở quán cà phê.*
- **It was Nam who** met Lan at the café yesterday.<br>*Chính Nam (chứ không phải ai khác) đã gặp Lan ở quán cà phê hôm qua.*
- **It was Lan that** Nam met at the café yesterday.<br>*Người Nam gặp ở quán cà phê hôm qua chính là Lan.*
- **It was at the café that** Nam met Lan yesterday.<br>*Chính ở quán cà phê mà Nam gặp Lan hôm qua.*
- **It was yesterday that** Nam met Lan at the café.<br>*Chính hôm qua Nam đã gặp Lan ở quán cà phê.*

More examples:

- **It is** my mother **who** cooks in our family.<br>*Trong nhà tôi, mẹ tôi mới là người nấu ăn.*
- **It was** the noise **that** woke me up.<br>*Chính tiếng ồn đã làm tôi tỉnh giấc.*
- **It is** maths, not English, **that** I find difficult.<br>*Môn tôi thấy khó là toán, chứ không phải tiếng Anh.*

## Emphasising the verb: do / does / did

Add **do / does / did** before the verb (the verb goes back to its **base form**) to mean "**really**, **truly**". People often do this to answer someone who doubts them. In speech, stress *do / does / did*.

- I **do** like your new haircut!<br>*Tôi thật sự thích kiểu tóc mới của bạn đấy!*
- She **does** work hard. She's just very quiet.<br>*Cô ấy làm việc chăm chỉ thật mà. Chỉ là cô ấy rất ít nói.*
- I **did** call you, but you didn't answer.<br>*Tôi có gọi cho bạn thật mà, nhưng bạn không nghe máy.*
- **Do** sit down and have some tea.<br>*Mời anh ngồi xuống uống chén trà đã.* (a warm invitation)

## Inversion

When certain words with a **negative** or **limiting** meaning start a sentence, we **move the auxiliary in front of the subject**, the same way we make a question (but this is **not** a question).

::: warning IMPORTANT
If the sentence has no auxiliary (present simple, past simple), **borrow do / does / did**, just like in a question:

We **rarely go** out. → Rarely **do we go** out.<br>
She **seldom cooked**. → Seldom **did she cook**.
:::

Each example below has the **normal sentence** on top and the **inverted sentence** below it.

### 1. Never / Rarely / Seldom — never, rarely

- I have never seen such a beautiful sunset.<br>→ **Never have I seen** such a beautiful sunset.<br>*Chưa bao giờ tôi thấy cảnh hoàng hôn nào đẹp như vậy.*
- I will never forget this day.<br>→ **Never will I forget** this day.<br>*Tôi sẽ không bao giờ quên ngày hôm nay.*
- We rarely go out at night.<br>→ **Rarely do we go** out at night.<br>*Chúng tôi hiếm khi ra ngoài buổi tối.*
- He seldom says sorry.<br>→ **Seldom does he say** sorry.<br>*Anh ta hiếm khi nói lời xin lỗi.*

### 2. Not only … but also — not only … but also

Invert the **Not only** part; the **but** part stays the same.

- She is not only beautiful but also kind.<br>→ **Not only is she** beautiful, **but** she is **also** kind.<br>*Cô ấy không những xinh đẹp mà còn tốt bụng.*
- He speaks not only English but also French.<br>→ **Not only does he speak** English, **but** he **also** speaks French.<br>*Anh ấy không những nói tiếng Anh mà còn nói tiếng Pháp.*
- She not only cooked dinner but also washed the dishes.<br>→ **Not only did she cook** dinner, **but** she **also** washed the dishes.<br>*Cô ấy không những nấu bữa tối mà còn rửa bát.*

### 3. No sooner … than / Hardly … when — as soon as … then

Use the **past perfect** (had V3) in the first part and the past simple in the second part. Remember the pairs: **No sooner … than**, **Hardly … when**.

- I had no sooner arrived home than it started to rain.<br>→ **No sooner had I arrived** home **than** it started to rain.<br>*Tôi vừa về đến nhà thì trời đổ mưa.*
- We had no sooner started eating than the lights went out.<br>→ **No sooner had we started** eating **than** the lights went out.<br>*Chúng tôi vừa bắt đầu ăn thì mất điện.*
- He had hardly sat down when the phone rang.<br>→ **Hardly had he sat** down **when** the phone rang.<br>*Anh ấy vừa ngồi xuống thì điện thoại reo.*
- She had hardly left the house when it started to snow.<br>→ **Hardly had she left** the house **when** it started to snow.<br>*Cô ấy vừa ra khỏi nhà thì tuyết bắt đầu rơi.*

### 4. Only when / Only after — only when, only after

Invert the **second part** (the main clause), not the part right after *when / after*.

- I only understood the problem when I read the letter.<br>→ **Only when** I read the letter **did I understand** the problem.<br>*Chỉ khi đọc lá thư, tôi mới hiểu ra vấn đề.*
- You can leave only after you finish your work.<br>→ **Only after** you finish your work **can you** leave.<br>*Chỉ sau khi làm xong việc, bạn mới được về.*
- I realised how much I loved her only after she left.<br>→ **Only after** she left **did I realise** how much I loved her.<br>*Chỉ sau khi cô ấy ra đi, tôi mới nhận ra mình yêu cô ấy nhiều thế nào.*
- We only knew the truth when he told us.<br>→ **Only when** he told us **did we know** the truth.<br>*Chỉ khi anh ấy kể, chúng tôi mới biết sự thật.*

### 5. So + adjective … that — so … that

Move **So + adjective** to the front, then **be**, and then the subject.

- The test was so difficult that nobody passed.<br>→ **So difficult was the test that** nobody passed.<br>*Bài kiểm tra khó đến nỗi không ai qua.*
- The music was so loud that we couldn't talk.<br>→ **So loud was the music that** we couldn't talk.<br>*Nhạc to đến nỗi chúng tôi không nói chuyện được.*
- She was so tired that she fell asleep immediately.<br>→ **So tired was she that** she fell asleep immediately.<br>*Cô ấy mệt đến nỗi ngủ thiếp đi ngay.*

### Inversion summary table

| Starts with | Where to invert | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Never / Rarely / Seldom | right after that word | **Rarely does she** cook.<br>*Cô ấy hiếm khi nấu ăn.* |
| Not only | the *Not only* part | **Not only is it** cheap, but it is also good.<br>*Nó không những rẻ mà còn tốt.* |
| No sooner / Hardly | right after that word, with *had V3* | **No sooner had we** sat down **than** the film started.<br>*Chúng tôi vừa ngồi xuống thì phim bắt đầu.* |
| Only when / Only after | the second part | Only when it rained **did we go** inside.<br>*Chỉ khi trời mưa chúng tôi mới vào trong.* |
| So + adjective | right after the adjective | **So hot was the day that** we stayed home.<br>*Trời nóng đến nỗi chúng tôi ở nhà.* |

## Common mistakes

::: danger COMMON MISTAKES
| ✗ Wrong | ✓ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Never I have seen such a big fish. | Never **have I** seen such a big fish. | When *Never* starts the sentence, you must invert the auxiliary. |
| Rarely we go out at night. | Rarely **do we go** out at night. | With no auxiliary, borrow *do*. |
| No sooner had I arrived when it rained. | No sooner had I arrived **than** it rained. | *No sooner* goes with *than*; only *Hardly* goes with *when*. |
| Only when I read the letter I understood. | Only when I read the letter **did I understand**. | Invert the second part. |
| It was Nam which broke the glass. | It was Nam **who / that** broke the glass. | For people, use *who / that*. |
:::

## In context

> **Reporter:** What was your trip to Ha Long Bay like? \
> **Mrs. Thu:** Wonderful! **Never have I seen** such beautiful islands. \
> **Reporter:** Was the weather good? \
> **Mrs. Thu:** Not at first. **No sooner had we got** on the boat **than** it started to rain. But **it was** the rain **that** made the bay look so special. \
> **Reporter:** Would you go again? \
> **Mrs. Thu:** I **do** hope so. **Not only is** the bay beautiful, **but** the seafood is **also** fantastic.

::: details Vietnamese translation
**Phóng viên:** Chuyến đi vịnh Hạ Long của bà thế nào ạ?<br>
**Bà Thu:** Tuyệt vời! Chưa bao giờ tôi thấy những hòn đảo đẹp như thế.<br>
**Phóng viên:** Thời tiết có đẹp không ạ?<br>
**Bà Thu:** Lúc đầu thì không. Chúng tôi vừa lên thuyền thì trời đổ mưa. Nhưng chính cơn mưa lại làm cho vịnh trông đặc biệt hơn.<br>
**Phóng viên:** Bà có muốn quay lại nữa không ạ?<br>
**Bà Thu:** Tôi thật sự mong là có. Vịnh không những đẹp mà hải sản còn rất ngon.
:::

## Remember

::: warning SUMMARY
- Cleft sentence: **It is / was + emphasised part + who / that …**
- Emphasising the verb: **do / does / did + base verb**.
- A negative / limiting word at the start → **auxiliary + S + V** (like a question); with no auxiliary, borrow **do / does / did**.
- Remember the pairs: **No sooner … than**, **Hardly … when**; **Only when / after** inverts the second part.
:::
