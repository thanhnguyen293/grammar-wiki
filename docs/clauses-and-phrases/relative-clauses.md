# Relative clauses

A relative clause is a "small sentence" that comes right after a noun to show **which person, which thing, or which place** you mean. It lets you join two short sentences into one longer, more natural sentence, instead of saying them separately.

## Formula

::: info FORMULA
| Form | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Replaces the subject | Noun + who / which / that + V | The man **who lives** next door is a doctor.<br>*Người đàn ông sống ở nhà bên cạnh là bác sĩ.* |
| Replaces the object | Noun + (whom / which / that) + S + V | The cake **(that) my mom made** was delicious.<br>*Cái bánh mẹ tôi làm rất ngon.* |
| Possession | Noun + whose + noun + V | I have a friend **whose father** is a pilot.<br>*Tôi có một người bạn có bố là phi công.* |
| Place, time, reason | Noun + where / when / why + S + V | This is the house **where I was born**.<br>*Đây là ngôi nhà nơi tôi sinh ra.* |
:::

::: warning IMPORTANT
A relative clause always comes **right after the noun** it describes. If that noun is in the middle of the sentence, the relative clause goes in the middle of the sentence too.

The phone **which I bought yesterday** was expensive. *(Cái điện thoại tôi mua hôm qua rất đắt.)*
:::

## How to join two sentences into one

Follow 3 steps:

1. Find the **shared word** in the two sentences (it means the same person or thing).
2. In the second sentence, replace that shared word with the right **relative pronoun** (who, which, whose, where…).
3. Move the relative pronoun to the **start** of the second sentence, then put that whole sentence **right after** the noun in the first sentence.

**Example 1 — a person, as the subject → who**

- Sentence 1: I have a friend.<br>*Tôi có một người bạn.*
- Sentence 2: **She** speaks Japanese.<br>*Cô ấy nói được tiếng Nhật.*
- *She* is the same as *a friend*. It is a person and it is in the subject position → use **who**.
- Joined sentence: I have a friend **who speaks Japanese**.<br>*Tôi có một người bạn nói được tiếng Nhật.*

**Example 2 — a thing, as the object → which / that**

- Sentence 1: The phone was expensive.<br>*Cái điện thoại đó rất đắt.*
- Sentence 2: I bought **it** yesterday.<br>*Tôi mua nó hôm qua.*
- *it* is the same as *the phone*. It is a thing and it comes after the verb (the object) → use **which** or **that**, then remove *it*.
- Joined sentence: The phone **which I bought yesterday** was expensive.<br>*Cái điện thoại tôi mua hôm qua rất đắt.*

**Example 3 — possession → whose**

- Sentence 1: That is the woman.<br>*Đó là người phụ nữ ấy.*
- Sentence 2: **Her** son is my classmate.<br>*Con trai bà ấy là bạn cùng lớp của tôi.*
- *Her* shows possession → use **whose**.
- Joined sentence: That is the woman **whose son is my classmate**.<br>*Đó là người phụ nữ có con trai học cùng lớp với tôi.*

**Example 4 — a place → where**

- Sentence 1: This is the café.<br>*Đây là quán cà phê đó.*
- Sentence 2: We first met **there**.<br>*Chúng ta gặp nhau lần đầu ở đó.*
- *there* shows a place → use **where**, and remove *there*.
- Joined sentence: This is the café **where we first met**.<br>*Đây là quán cà phê nơi chúng ta gặp nhau lần đầu.*

## How to use

### 1. who — for people, as the subject

- The woman **who works** in that shop is very friendly.<br>*Người phụ nữ làm ở cửa hàng đó rất thân thiện.*
- I don't like people **who talk** too much.<br>*Tôi không thích những người nói quá nhiều.*
- The boy **who won** the race is my nephew.<br>*Cậu bé thắng cuộc đua là cháu tôi.*
- Do you know anyone **who can fix** a computer?<br>*Bạn có quen ai biết sửa máy tính không?*

### 2. whom — for people, as the object

- The woman **whom I met** yesterday is my new boss.<br>*Người phụ nữ tôi gặp hôm qua là sếp mới của tôi.*
- He is a teacher **whom everyone loves**.<br>*Thầy ấy là người thầy mà ai cũng quý.*
- The girl **whom you saw** at the party is my sister.<br>*Cô gái bạn thấy ở bữa tiệc là em gái tôi.*

::: tip TIP
**whom** sounds quite formal. In everyday speech, people use **who**, **that**, or leave the pronoun out: The woman **I met** yesterday is my new boss.
:::

### 3. which — for things and animals

- I lost the umbrella **which my mother gave me**.<br>*Tôi làm mất chiếc ô mẹ tôi tặng.*
- This is the bus **which goes** to the city centre.<br>*Đây là xe buýt đi vào trung tâm thành phố.*
- We have a dog **which barks** all night.<br>*Nhà chúng tôi có một con chó sủa suốt đêm.*

### 4. that — for both people and things

- She is the nurse **that helped** my father.<br>*Cô ấy là y tá đã giúp bố tôi.*
- Where is the money **that I left** on the table?<br>*Số tiền tôi để trên bàn đâu rồi?*
- The film **that we watched** last night was very funny.<br>*Bộ phim chúng tôi xem tối qua rất buồn cười.*

::: tip TIP
After a **superlative**, **all**, **everything**, **nothing**, and **the only**, people usually use **that**:
- It's the best film **that** I've ever seen.<br>*Đó là bộ phim hay nhất tôi từng xem.*
- Everything **that** he said was true.<br>*Mọi điều anh ấy nói đều là sự thật.*
:::

### 5. whose — possession (that person's / that thing's)

**whose** is always followed by a **noun**.

- I have a friend **whose father** is a pilot.<br>*Tôi có một người bạn có bố là phi công.*
- That's the man **whose car** was stolen.<br>*Đó là người đàn ông bị trộm mất xe hơi.*
- We stayed in a hotel **whose rooms** were very small.<br>*Chúng tôi ở một khách sạn có phòng rất nhỏ.*

### 6. where — for places

- This is the house **where I was born**.<br>*Đây là ngôi nhà nơi tôi sinh ra.*
- Do you know a shop **where I can buy** cheap shoes?<br>*Bạn có biết cửa hàng nào bán giày rẻ không?*
- The village **where my grandparents live** is very quiet.<br>*Ngôi làng nơi ông bà tôi sống rất yên tĩnh.*

### 7. when — for time

- I'll never forget the day **when I met you**.<br>*Anh sẽ không bao giờ quên ngày anh gặp em.*
- Summer is the season **when many people go** to the beach.<br>*Mùa hè là mùa nhiều người đi biển.*
- Do you remember the time **when we got lost** in Da Lat?<br>*Bạn còn nhớ lần chúng ta bị lạc ở Đà Lạt không?*

### 8. why — for reasons (after *the reason*)

- That's the reason **why I left** my job.<br>*Đó là lý do tôi nghỉ việc.*
- Tell me the reason **why you were** late.<br>*Hãy nói cho tôi biết lý do bạn đến muộn.*
- I don't know the reason **why she is** angry.<br>*Tôi không biết lý do cô ấy giận.*

### Which relative pronoun should you choose?

| Word | Used for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| who | people (subject) | the girl **who** sings<br>*cô gái hát* |
| whom | people (object) | the girl **whom** I love<br>*cô gái tôi yêu* |
| which | things, animals | the book **which** I read<br>*cuốn sách tôi đọc* |
| that | people or things | the man **that** called you<br>*người đàn ông đã gọi bạn* |
| whose | possession + noun | the man **whose** wife is a singer<br>*người đàn ông có vợ là ca sĩ* |
| where | places | the city **where** I work<br>*thành phố nơi tôi làm việc* |
| when | time | the day **when** we met<br>*ngày chúng ta gặp nhau* |
| why | reasons | the reason **why** he cried<br>*lý do anh ấy khóc* |

## Defining and non-defining clauses

| Type | Features | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Defining | Needed to know **which person / thing** you mean. No commas. You can use **that**. | The students **who passed** the test got a prize.<br>*Những học sinh đỗ bài kiểm tra được thưởng.* (only the ones who passed) |
| Non-defining | Just **extra information**; the sentence is still clear without it. Uses **commas**. Do **not** use that. | My mother, **who is 60 this year**, still goes to work.<br>*Mẹ tôi, năm nay 60 tuổi, vẫn đi làm.* |

A comma can change the meaning of the whole sentence:

- My brother **who lives in Da Nang** is a teacher.<br>*Người anh trai sống ở Đà Nẵng của tôi là giáo viên.* (I have several brothers; this is the one who lives in Da Nang)
- My brother, **who lives in Da Nang**, is a teacher.<br>*Anh trai tôi, người sống ở Đà Nẵng, là giáo viên.* (I have only one brother)

Non-defining clauses often come after **names** and after people or things there is only one of:

- Hanoi, **which is the capital of Vietnam**, has many old streets.<br>*Hà Nội, thủ đô của Việt Nam, có nhiều phố cổ.*
- I met Mr. Nam, **whose daughter is a famous singer**.<br>*Tôi đã gặp ông Nam, người có con gái là ca sĩ nổi tiếng.*
- We visited Hoi An, **where we bought some lovely lanterns**.<br>*Chúng tôi đến Hội An, nơi chúng tôi mua được mấy chiếc đèn lồng rất đẹp.*

::: warning IMPORTANT
Never use **that** after a **comma**.

✗ My father, **that** is a doctor, works very hard.<br>
✓ My father, **who** is a doctor, works very hard. *(Bố tôi, là bác sĩ, làm việc rất vất vả.)*
:::

## Leaving out the relative pronoun

When the relative pronoun is the **object** (another subject comes after it) in a **defining** clause, you can leave it out. This is very common in speech.

- The book **(that) I'm reading** is very interesting.<br>*Cuốn sách tôi đang đọc rất hay.*
- The man **(who) we met** at the party is my boss.<br>*Người đàn ông chúng ta gặp ở bữa tiệc là sếp tôi.*
- Is this the bag **(which) you lost**?<br>*Đây có phải cái túi bạn làm mất không?*
- The food **(that) she cooks** is always delicious.<br>*Món ăn cô ấy nấu lúc nào cũng ngon.*

You **cannot** leave it out when the pronoun is the **subject** (a verb comes right after it):

- The man **who called** you is my boss.<br>*Người đàn ông gọi cho bạn là sếp tôi.* (you cannot remove *who*)

## Reduced relative clauses

When the relative pronoun is the subject, you can shorten the clause.

| Meaning | How to shorten | Full sentence → short sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Active (the person / thing **does the action**) | remove the pronoun + be, change the verb → **V-ing** | The girl **who is sitting** next to me → The girl **sitting** next to me is my cousin.<br>*Cô gái ngồi cạnh tôi là em họ tôi.* |
| Passive (something **is done to** the person / thing) | remove the pronoun + be, keep **V3** | The cake **which was made** by my mom → The cake **made** by my mom was delicious.<br>*Cái bánh do mẹ tôi làm rất ngon.* |

More examples with **V-ing** (active):

- People **living** in the city often feel stressed. (= who live)<br>*Những người sống ở thành phố thường hay căng thẳng.*
- The bus **going** to the airport leaves every 30 minutes. (= which goes)<br>*Xe buýt ra sân bay cứ 30 phút có một chuyến.*
- Who is the man **wearing** a black hat? (= who is wearing)<br>*Người đàn ông đội mũ đen là ai vậy?*

More examples with **V3** (passive):

- Most of the clothes **sold** in this shop are cheap. (= which are sold)<br>*Phần lớn quần áo bán ở cửa hàng này đều rẻ.*
- Cars **made** in Japan are very popular here. (= which are made)<br>*Xe hơi sản xuất ở Nhật rất được ưa chuộng ở đây.*
- The man **injured** in the accident is now in hospital. (= who was injured)<br>*Người đàn ông bị thương trong vụ tai nạn hiện đang nằm viện.*

## Preposition + whom / which

When a verb goes with a preposition (*talk to, live in, travel with…*), the preposition can go at the **end of the clause** (everyday speech) or **before whom / which** (formal writing).

- The man **to whom I spoke** was very kind.<br>= The man **(who) I spoke to** was very kind.<br>*Người đàn ông tôi nói chuyện cùng rất tử tế.*
- The house **in which they live** is very old.<br>= The house **(that) they live in** is very old.<br>*Ngôi nhà họ đang sống rất cũ.*
- This is the friend **with whom I travelled** to Japan.<br>= This is the friend **I travelled to Japan with**.<br>*Đây là người bạn đã đi Nhật cùng tôi.*
- The chair **on which she was sitting** was broken.<br>= The chair **she was sitting on** was broken.<br>*Cái ghế cô ấy đang ngồi bị gãy.*

::: tip TIP
Right after a preposition, use only **whom** (people) or **which** (things), **not** *who* or *that*. Also, *in which* for a place can be replaced by **where**: the house **where** they live.
:::

## Common mistakes

::: danger COMMON MISTAKES
| ✗ Wrong | ✓ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The man which lives next door is a doctor. | The man **who** lives next door is a doctor. | For people, use *who / that*, not *which*. |
| This is the book that I bought it yesterday. | This is the book that I bought yesterday. | *that* already replaces *it*, so don't repeat *it*. |
| My father, that is a doctor, works hard. | My father, **who** is a doctor, works hard. | Don't use *that* after a comma. |
| The man to who I spoke was kind. | The man to **whom** I spoke was kind. | After a preposition, use *whom*. |
| This is the house where I bought last year. | This is the house **which** I bought last year. | *bought* needs an object (bought what?), so use *which / that*, not *where*. |
:::

## In context

> **Hoa:** Who's the woman **who is standing** next to you in this photo? \
> **Nam:** That's my aunt Mai, **who lives in Australia**. And the boy **wearing a red T-shirt** is her son. \
> **Hoa:** Where did you take it? \
> **Nam:** At the beach **where we spent our summer holiday**. Look, that's the hotel **we stayed at**. \
> **Hoa:** It looks lovely! Was it expensive? \
> **Nam:** No. My aunt has a friend **whose family owns it**, so we got a good price.

## Remember

::: warning SUMMARY
- **who** for people, **which** for things, **that** for both; **whose** + noun for possession; **where / when / why** for places, time, and reasons.
- A **comma** = extra information: don't use *that*, and you can't leave out the pronoun.
- If the pronoun is the **object**, you can leave it out: the book **(that)** I read.
- Reduced clauses: active → **V-ing**, passive → **V3**.
:::
