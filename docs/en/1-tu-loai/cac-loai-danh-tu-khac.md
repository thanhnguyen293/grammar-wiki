# Other ways to classify nouns

Besides countable / uncountable, grammar books also group nouns in a few other ways: common and proper, concrete and abstract, collective, and compound. Use this page as a reference when you meet these names.

::: tip TIP
The classification that **really matters** when you speak and write is **countable or uncountable**, because it decides whether you use *a/an*, the plural, *many* or *much*. For the groups below, you only need to understand the names. Study the lesson [Countable and uncountable nouns](./dem-duoc-khong-dem-duoc) carefully.
:::

## Summary table

| Type | Example | What to remember |
|---|---|---|
| Common noun | *city, teacher, phone*<br>I live in a big **city**.<br>*Tôi sống ở một thành phố lớn.* | Written in lowercase; can use *a / an / the*. |
| Proper noun | *Lan, Hanoi, Monday, January*<br>**Lan** was born in **January**.<br>*Lan sinh vào tháng Một.* | Always **capitalize** the first letter, including days, months and languages (*English*). Usually no *a / an / the*. |
| Concrete noun | *table, rice, dog*<br>The **dog** is under the **table**.<br>*Con chó ở dưới gầm bàn.* | Something you can see, hear or touch. |
| Abstract noun | *love, happiness, advice, time*<br>Thank you for your **advice**.<br>*Cảm ơn lời khuyên của bạn.* | You can't see or touch it. Most are **uncountable**: don't say *an advice*. |
| Collective noun | *family, team, class, staff*<br>My **family** is very big.<br>*Gia đình tôi rất đông người.* | Names a group; usually takes a **singular** verb. But *police* is always plural. |
| Compound noun | *bus stop, toothbrush, mother-in-law*<br>I'll wait at the **bus stop**.<br>*Tôi sẽ đợi ở trạm xe buýt.* | For the plural, add -s to the **main noun**: *bus stops, mothers-in-law*. |

## Common nouns and proper nouns

A common noun is the general name for a kind of person or thing. A proper noun is the name of **one** specific person, place or day.

- My **sister** works in a **hospital**.<br>*Chị tôi làm việc ở bệnh viện.*
- My sister **Hoa** works at **Bach Mai Hospital**.<br>*Chị Hoa của tôi làm việc ở bệnh viện Bạch Mai.*
- We have English on **Tuesdays** and **Fridays**.<br>*Chúng tôi học tiếng Anh vào thứ Ba và thứ Sáu.*
- He speaks **Vietnamese** and **Japanese**.<br>*Anh ấy nói tiếng Việt và tiếng Nhật.*

## Concrete nouns and abstract nouns

Concrete nouns name things you can see and touch. Abstract nouns name feelings, ideas and qualities.

- There is a **cup** of **coffee** on the **desk**.<br>*Có một cốc cà phê trên bàn làm việc.*
- **Health** is more important than **money**.<br>*Sức khỏe quan trọng hơn tiền bạc.*
- I don't have much **time** today.<br>*Hôm nay tôi không có nhiều thời gian.*
- She gave me some good **advice**.<br>*Cô ấy cho tôi vài lời khuyên hay.*

## Collective nouns

A collective noun names **a group** of people or things as one unit.

- Our **team** wins most of its games.<br>*Đội chúng tôi thắng hầu hết các trận.*
- The **class** starts at 8 o'clock.<br>*Lớp học bắt đầu lúc 8 giờ.*
- My **family** lives in Can Tho.<br>*Gia đình tôi sống ở Cần Thơ.*
- The **police** are looking for the thief.<br>*Cảnh sát đang tìm tên trộm.*

::: tip TIP
British English sometimes uses a plural verb with collective nouns (*My family are...*). As a learner, it is safest to use the **singular**, except with *police* (always plural).
:::

## Compound nouns

A compound noun is two or more words that together make a new meaning. There are three ways to write them: **as one word** (*toothbrush, bedroom*), **as separate words** (*bus stop, credit card*), and **with hyphens** (*mother-in-law*).

- I need a new **toothbrush**.<br>*Tôi cần một cái bàn chải đánh răng mới.*
- There are two **bus stops** near my house.<br>*Gần nhà tôi có hai trạm xe buýt.*
- Can I pay by **credit card**?<br>*Tôi trả bằng thẻ tín dụng được không?*
- She bought a pair of shoes at the **shoe shop**.<br>*Cô ấy mua một đôi giày ở cửa hàng giày.*

::: tip TIP
The first noun works like an adjective, so it **stays singular**: *a shoe shop* (not *a shoes shop*), *a ten-minute walk*.
:::

## Common mistakes

::: danger COMMON MISTAKES
| ✗ Wrong | ✓ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| See you on monday. | See you on **Monday**. | Days and months are proper nouns, so capitalize them. |
| She gave me an advice. | She gave me **some advice**. | *advice* is abstract and uncountable. |
| I went to a shoes shop. | I went to a **shoe** shop. | The first noun stays singular. |
| two mother-in-laws | two **mothers-in-law** | Add -s to the main noun. |
| The police is coming. | The police **are** coming. | *police* always takes a plural verb. |
:::

## Remember

::: warning SUMMARY
- Proper nouns (names of people, places, days, months, languages) are always **capitalized**.
- Most abstract nouns (*advice, information, happiness*) are **uncountable**.
- Collective nouns usually take a **singular** verb, except *police*.
- Compound nouns: for the plural, add -s to the **main noun** (*bus stops, mothers-in-law*).
:::
