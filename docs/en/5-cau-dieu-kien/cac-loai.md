# Types of conditionals

A conditional sentence has two parts: the **If** part (the condition) and the **result** part. We use it to say "if… then…": something that always happens, something that may happen, or something that is only imagined and not true.

## Formula

::: info FORMULA
| Type | If part | Result part | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type 0 | If + present simple | present simple | If you **heat** ice, it **melts**. |
| Type 1 | If + present simple | will + V | If it **rains**, I **will stay** home. |
| Type 2 | If + past simple | would + V | If I **had** money, I **would buy** a car. |
| Type 3 | If + had + V3 | would have + V3 | If I **had studied**, I **would have passed**. |
| Mixed | If + had + V3 | would + V | If I **had slept** early, I **wouldn't be** tired now. |
:::

The If part can come first or second. When the If part comes **first**, put a comma between the two parts; when it comes **second**, you don't need a comma.

- **If it rains,** I will stay home.<br>*Nếu trời mưa, tôi sẽ ở nhà.*
- I will stay home **if it rains**.<br>*Tôi sẽ ở nhà nếu trời mưa.*

### The four types and real life

| Type | Condition | Time | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Always true, a fact | Any time | If you **don't water** plants, they **die**. |
| 1 | Can really happen | Present, future | If I **finish** early, I'**ll call** you. |
| 2 | Not real, imagined | Present | If I **lived** near the sea, I **would swim** every day. |
| 3 | Not real, already too late | Past | If we **had left** earlier, we **wouldn't have missed** the bus. |

## How to use

### 1. Type 0: facts, things that always happen

When the condition happens, the result is **always** the same. You can use *when* instead of *if* and the meaning does not change.

- If you **mix** red and yellow, you **get** orange.<br>*Nếu trộn màu đỏ và vàng, bạn sẽ có màu cam.*
- If I **drink** coffee at night, I **can't** sleep.<br>*Nếu tôi uống cà phê buổi tối, tôi không ngủ được.*
- If babies **are** hungry, they **cry**.<br>*Nếu em bé đói, chúng khóc.*
- Plants **die** if they **don't get** enough water.<br>*Cây sẽ chết nếu không có đủ nước.*

### 2. Type 1: things that can happen now or in the future

The condition **can really happen**. The result part uses *will*; you can also use *can, may, should* or an imperative.

- If it **rains** tomorrow, we **will cancel** the picnic.<br>*Nếu mai trời mưa, chúng tôi sẽ hủy buổi dã ngoại.*
- If you **study** hard, you **will pass** the exam.<br>*Nếu bạn học chăm, bạn sẽ đỗ kỳ thi.*
- If she **doesn't hurry**, she **will be** late for work.<br>*Nếu cô ấy không nhanh lên, cô ấy sẽ đi làm muộn.*
- If you **see** Nam, **tell** him to call me.<br>*Nếu bạn gặp Nam, bảo cậu ấy gọi cho tôi.*

### 3. Type 2: things that are not real now

You **imagine** something that is the opposite of the present. The verb in the If part moves back to the past, but the sentence is still about **now**.

- If I **had** a lot of money, I **would travel** around the world.<br>*Nếu tôi có nhiều tiền, tôi sẽ đi du lịch vòng quanh thế giới.*
- If my house **were** bigger, I **would invite** all my friends.<br>*Nếu nhà tôi rộng hơn, tôi sẽ mời tất cả bạn bè.*
- If he **spoke** English, he **could get** a better job.<br>*Nếu anh ấy nói được tiếng Anh, anh ấy có thể kiếm được việc tốt hơn.*
- If I **were** you, I **would see** a doctor.<br>*Nếu tôi là bạn, tôi sẽ đi khám bác sĩ.*

::: tip TIP
**If I were you, I would…** is a very common way to give advice: "If I were you, I would…".
:::

### 4. Type 3: things that were not real in the past

Talk about something that **already happened**, and imagine what would be different if it had gone another way. It often shows **regret** or **blame**.

- If I **had studied** harder, I **would have passed** the exam.<br>*Nếu tôi đã học chăm hơn, tôi đã đỗ kỳ thi rồi.*
- If we **had left** home earlier, we **wouldn't have missed** the train.<br>*Nếu chúng tôi rời nhà sớm hơn, chúng tôi đã không lỡ chuyến tàu.*
- If she **had known** your number, she **would have called** you.<br>*Nếu cô ấy biết số của bạn, cô ấy đã gọi cho bạn rồi.*
- If it **hadn't rained**, we **would have gone** to the beach.<br>*Nếu trời không mưa, chúng tôi đã đi biển rồi.*

### 5. Mixed conditionals: the past affects the present

The If part is about the **past** (type 3), and the result part is about the **present** (type 2). There is often *now* in the second part.

- If I **had slept** early last night, I **wouldn't be** tired now.<br>*Nếu tối qua tôi ngủ sớm, bây giờ tôi đã không mệt.*
- If she **had taken** the job, she **would live** in Hanoi now.<br>*Nếu cô ấy đã nhận công việc đó, bây giờ cô ấy đang sống ở Hà Nội.*
- If I **had saved** money, I **would have** a car now.<br>*Nếu tôi đã tiết kiệm tiền, bây giờ tôi đã có xe hơi.*
- If he **hadn't eaten** so much, he **wouldn't feel** sick now.<br>*Nếu anh ấy không ăn nhiều như vậy, bây giờ anh ấy đã không thấy khó chịu.*

## The truth behind type 2 and type 3

Type 2 and type 3 sentences are always the **opposite** of the truth. To understand the sentence, "flip" it: positive becomes negative, negative becomes positive.

| Conditional sentence | The truth is | Example of the truth |
|---|---|---|
| If I **had** money, I would buy it. | I **don't have** money (now). | I **don't have** money, so I **won't buy** it. |
| If he **weren't** busy, he would come. | He **is busy**. | He **is** busy, so he **won't come**. |
| If I **knew** her address, I would visit her. | I **don't know** her address. | I **don't know** her address. |
| If you **had told** me, I would have helped. | You **didn't** tell me. | You **didn't tell** me, so I **didn't help**. |
| If we **hadn't been** late, we would have seen the start. | We **were** late. | We **were** late, so we **didn't see** the start. |
| If I **had slept** early, I wouldn't be tired now. | I **didn't** sleep early, and I'm tired **now**. | I **didn't sleep** early, so I **am** tired now. |

The other direction is also common in sentence-rewriting exercises:

- I **don't have** a car, so I **can't** drive you home.<br>→ If I **had** a car, I **could drive** you home.<br>*Nếu tôi có xe hơi, tôi có thể chở bạn về nhà.*
- She **didn't come** to the party because she **was** ill.<br>→ If she **hadn't been** ill, she **would have come** to the party.<br>*Nếu cô ấy không ốm, cô ấy đã đến bữa tiệc.*
- I **don't know** the answer, so I **can't** help you.<br>→ If I **knew** the answer, I **could help** you.<br>*Nếu tôi biết câu trả lời, tôi có thể giúp bạn.*

## The "one tense back" trick

From type 1 to type 2 to type 3, each part **moves one step back** into the past.

| Type | If part | Result part | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (possible) | rains | will stay | If it **rains**, I **will stay** home. |
| 2 (not real, present) | rained | would stay | If it **rained**, I **would stay** home. |
| 3 (not real, past) | had rained | would have stayed | If it **had rained**, I **would have stayed** home. |

Another example with the same situation:

- If I **see** him, I **will tell** him. *(I may see him)*<br>*Nếu tôi gặp anh ấy, tôi sẽ nói với anh ấy.*
- If I **saw** him, I **would tell** him. *(unlikely, just imagining)*<br>*Nếu tôi mà gặp anh ấy, tôi sẽ nói với anh ấy.*
- If I **had seen** him, I **would have told** him. *(I didn't see him)*<br>*Nếu tôi đã gặp anh ấy, tôi đã nói với anh ấy rồi.*

::: warning IMPORTANT
**Don't use *will* or *would* in the If part.** The If part uses the present simple (type 1), the past simple (type 2), or the past perfect (type 3).

If it ~~will rain~~ **rains**, I will stay home. · If I ~~would have~~ **had** time, I would help.
:::

## Were for every subject

In type 2 sentences, the verb *be* in the If part usually takes **were** for every subject, even *I, he, she, it*. In speaking you also often hear *was*, but in exams it is better to use **were**.

| Subject | Example |
|---|---|
| I | If I **were** rich, I would buy a big house. |
| He / She | If she **were** here, she would know what to do. |
| It | If it **were** cheaper, I would buy it. |
| You / We / They | If they **were** free, they would help us. |

- If I **were** taller, I would play basketball.<br>*Nếu tôi cao hơn, tôi sẽ chơi bóng rổ.*
- If my father **were** here, he would fix it.<br>*Nếu bố tôi ở đây, ông ấy sẽ sửa được nó.*
- If it **weren't** so cold, we would go out.<br>*Nếu trời không lạnh như vậy, chúng ta sẽ ra ngoài.*

## Common mistakes

::: danger COMMON MISTAKES
| ✗ Wrong | ✓ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| If it will rain, I will stay home. | If it **rains**, I will stay home. | Don't use *will* in the If part. |
| If I have money, I would buy a car. | If I **had** money, I would buy a car. | If the result part has *would*, the If part must move back to the past. |
| If I would know, I would tell you. | If I **knew**, I would tell you. | Don't use *would* in the If part. |
| If I had studied, I would pass. | If I had studied, I **would have passed**. | Type 3 (the past) needs *would have + V3*. |
| If he was me, he would agree. | If he **were** me, he would agree. | Type 2 should use *were* for every subject. |
:::

## In context

> **Hoa:** Are you going to the beach this weekend? \
> **Tuấn:** Maybe. If the weather **is** nice, we'**ll go** on Saturday. \
> **Hoa:** Lucky you! I'd love to come, but I have to work. If I **didn't have** to work, I **would come** with you. \
> **Tuấn:** That's a pity. Last month we went to Da Nang, but it rained all weekend. \
> **Hoa:** Oh no! If you **had checked** the weather, you **wouldn't have gone**. \
> **Tuấn:** You're right. And if I **hadn't spent** so much on that trip, I **would have** more money now!

::: details Vietnamese translation
**Hoa:** Cuối tuần này bạn đi biển à?<br>
**Tuấn:** Có thể. Nếu thời tiết đẹp, bọn mình sẽ đi vào thứ Bảy.<br>
**Hoa:** Sướng thế! Mình cũng muốn đi lắm, nhưng mình phải làm việc. Nếu không phải làm thì mình sẽ đi cùng các bạn.<br>
**Tuấn:** Tiếc thật. Tháng trước bọn mình đi Đà Nẵng mà mưa suốt cả cuối tuần.<br>
**Hoa:** Ôi không! Nếu bạn xem dự báo thời tiết trước, chắc bạn đã không đi rồi.<br>
**Tuấn:** Đúng vậy. Mà nếu mình không tiêu nhiều tiền cho chuyến đó, thì bây giờ mình đã có nhiều tiền hơn rồi!
:::

## Remember

::: warning SUMMARY
- **Type 0**: facts (present + present). **Type 1**: can happen (present + will).
- **Type 2**: not real now (past + would). **Type 3**: not real in the past (had V3 + would have V3).
- Type 2 and 3 sentences are always **the opposite of the truth**: flip positive to negative to understand them.
- The If part has **no will / would**; type 2 uses **were** for every subject.
:::
