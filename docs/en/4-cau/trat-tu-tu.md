# Word order

English puts words in a fairly fixed order: **who – does what – to what – where – when**. If you put a word in the wrong place, the sentence sounds strange or is hard to understand, so this is the "frame" for every sentence you say and write.

## Formula

::: info FORMULA
| Form | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Basic sentence | S + V + O + place + time | I met my friend **at the café yesterday**.<br>*Hôm qua tôi gặp bạn ở quán cà phê.* |
| With manner | S + V + (O) + manner + place + time | She sang **beautifully at the party last night**.<br>*Tối qua cô ấy hát rất hay ở bữa tiệc.* |
:::

### Putting each part in the right place

| Who (S) | Does what (V) | What / whom (O) | Where | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | bought | a new phone | at the mall | yesterday. |
| My mother | cooks | dinner | at home | every evening. |
| We | watched | a film | at the cinema | last night. |
| They | will visit | their grandparents | in Hue | next week. |

- I bought a new phone at the mall yesterday.<br>*Hôm qua tôi mua một chiếc điện thoại mới ở trung tâm thương mại.*
- My mother cooks dinner at home every evening.<br>*Tối nào mẹ tôi cũng nấu cơm ở nhà.*
- We watched a film at the cinema last night.<br>*Tối qua chúng tôi xem phim ở rạp.*
- They will visit their grandparents in Hue next week.<br>*Tuần sau họ sẽ về Huế thăm ông bà.*

::: tip TIP
Vietnamese often puts the time first ("**Hôm qua** tôi gặp…"), but English usually puts the time at the **end of the sentence**. You can still move the time to the front for emphasis; just remember to add a comma: **Yesterday**, I met my friend at the café.
:::

## The 5 basic sentence patterns

Almost every English sentence follows one of the 5 patterns below. (S = subject, V = verb, O = object, C = complement: a word that tells you what the subject or object is, or what it is like.)

### 1. S + V

The verb doesn't need an object.

- The baby **is sleeping**.<br>*Em bé đang ngủ.*
- She **smiled**.<br>*Cô ấy mỉm cười.*
- The bus **arrived** late.<br>*Xe buýt đến muộn.*
- Prices **went up** last month.<br>*Tháng trước giá cả tăng lên.*

### 2. S + V + O

The verb needs an object: does **what**, to **whom**.

- I **love** music.<br>*Tôi yêu âm nhạc.*
- She **bought** a dress.<br>*Cô ấy mua một chiếc váy.*
- We **are watching** TV.<br>*Chúng tôi đang xem ti vi.*
- My son **broke** the window.<br>*Con trai tôi làm vỡ cửa kính.*

### 3. S + V + C

After linking verbs like **be, look, feel, seem, become, taste, sound**, the complement tells you **what** the subject is or **what it is like**.

- My father **is** a doctor.<br>*Bố tôi là bác sĩ.*
- You **look** tired.<br>*Trông bạn mệt mỏi.*
- The soup **tastes** good.<br>*Món súp ngon.*
- She **became** a teacher.<br>*Cô ấy đã trở thành giáo viên.*

::: tip TIP
After these linking verbs, use an **adjective**, not an adverb: You look **tired** (not *tiredly*), The soup tastes **good** (not *well*).
:::

### 4. S + V + O + O

Verbs like **give, send, buy, show, tell, lend** have two objects: the **person who receives** comes first, the **thing** comes second. If you put the thing first, add **to** or **for** before the person.

- My dad **gave me a watch**.<br>= My dad gave a watch **to** me.<br>*Bố tôi tặng tôi một chiếc đồng hồ.*
- Can you **send me the photos**?<br>*Bạn gửi tôi mấy tấm ảnh được không?*
- She **bought her son a bike**.<br>= She bought a bike **for** her son.<br>*Cô ấy mua cho con trai một chiếc xe đạp.*
- I **told the children a story**.<br>*Tôi kể cho bọn trẻ nghe một câu chuyện.*

### 5. S + V + O + C

The complement tells you **what** the object is or **what it becomes**.

- They **named their baby Minh**.<br>*Họ đặt tên con là Minh.*
- The news **made her happy**.<br>*Tin đó làm cô ấy vui.*
- Please **keep the room clean**.<br>*Hãy giữ phòng sạch sẽ.*
- We **found the test easy**.<br>*Chúng tôi thấy bài kiểm tra dễ.*

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| S + V | The children **laughed**.<br>*Bọn trẻ cười.* |
| S + V + O | I **drink** coffee.<br>*Tôi uống cà phê.* |
| S + V + C | The room **is** dark.<br>*Căn phòng tối.* |
| S + V + O + O | He **showed** me his house.<br>*Anh ấy cho tôi xem nhà của anh ấy.* |
| S + V + O + C | The film **made** me sad.<br>*Bộ phim làm tôi buồn.* |

## Adjective order (OSASCOMP)

Vietnamese puts adjectives **after** the noun ("áo **đỏ**"), but English puts them **before** it ("a **red** shirt"). When there are several adjectives, they go in this order:

| Order | Type | Example words |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **O**pinion | opinion, judgement | beautiful, nice, ugly, cheap<br>*đẹp, hay, xấu, rẻ* |
| 2. **S**ize | size | big, small, long, tall<br>*to, nhỏ, dài, cao* |
| 3. **A**ge | age, how new or old | old, new, young<br>*cũ / già, mới, trẻ* |
| 4. **S**hape | shape | round, square, flat<br>*tròn, vuông, phẳng* |
| 5. **C**olour | colour | red, black, white<br>*đỏ, đen, trắng* |
| 6. **O**rigin | where it comes from | Vietnamese, Japanese, Italian<br>*Việt Nam, Nhật, Ý* |
| 7. **M**aterial | what it is made of | wooden, cotton, silk, leather<br>*gỗ, bông, lụa, da* |
| 8. **P**urpose | what it is for | sleeping (bag), running (shoes)<br>*(túi) ngủ, (giày) chạy bộ* |

- a **beautiful old** house (opinion + age)<br>*một ngôi nhà cổ đẹp*
- a **small round wooden** table (size + shape + material)<br>*một chiếc bàn gỗ tròn nhỏ*
- a **beautiful long silk** dress (opinion + size + material)<br>*một chiếc váy lụa dài rất đẹp*
- an **old black leather** bag (age + colour + material)<br>*một chiếc túi da màu đen cũ*
- a **cheap Japanese** car (opinion + origin)<br>*một chiếc xe hơi Nhật giá rẻ*
- a **new blue sleeping** bag (age + colour + purpose)<br>*một chiếc túi ngủ màu xanh mới*

::: tip TIP
The English order is almost the **opposite** of Vietnamese: *một chiếc bàn **gỗ tròn nhỏ*** → *a **small round wooden** table*. In everyday speech, people rarely use more than 2–3 adjectives at once.
:::

## Where adverbs go

| Type of adverb | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency (always, often, never) | before a main verb, after *be* and auxiliary verbs | She **often** walks to work.<br>*Cô ấy thường đi bộ đi làm.* |
| Manner (slowly, carefully, well) | after the verb, or after the object | She speaks English **well**.<br>*Cô ấy nói tiếng Anh giỏi.* |
| Place (here, outside, at home) | end of the sentence | The children are playing **outside**.<br>*Bọn trẻ đang chơi ngoài sân.* |
| Time (now, yesterday, last week) | end of the sentence, or the start for emphasis | I saw him **yesterday**.<br>*Hôm qua tôi gặp anh ấy.* |
| Degree (very, really, quite) | before an adjective, adverb, or verb | The film was **very** good.<br>*Bộ phim rất hay.* |

More examples:

- He is **never** late.<br>*Anh ấy không bao giờ đến muộn.* (after *be*)
- I have **always** wanted to go to Japan.<br>*Tôi luôn muốn đến Nhật Bản.* (after the auxiliary *have*)
- Please drive **carefully**.<br>*Làm ơn lái xe cẩn thận.*
- I **really** like your new haircut.<br>*Tôi rất thích kiểu tóc mới của bạn.*

::: warning IMPORTANT
Don't put an adverb **between the verb and its object**.

✗ She speaks **very well** English.<br>
✓ She speaks English **very well**. *(Cô ấy nói tiếng Anh rất giỏi.)*
:::

## Place comes before time

When a sentence has both a place and a time, the **place** comes first.

- I was **at home all day**.<br>*Tôi ở nhà cả ngày.*
- We're going **to Da Nang next month**.<br>*Tháng sau chúng tôi sẽ đi Đà Nẵng.*
- She arrived **at the airport at 6 a.m.**<br>*Cô ấy đến sân bay lúc 6 giờ sáng.*
- They have lived **in this house since 2015**.<br>*Họ sống ở ngôi nhà này từ năm 2015.*

::: tip TIP
When there are several times or places, the **smaller** unit comes before the **bigger** one: at 7 o'clock **on Monday** *(lúc 7 giờ ngày thứ Hai)*, in a small village **in Nghe An** *(ở một ngôi làng nhỏ tại Nghệ An)*.
:::

## Common mistakes

::: danger COMMON MISTAKES
| ✗ Wrong | ✓ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I yesterday met him. | I met him **yesterday**. | Time goes at the end (or the start) of the sentence. |
| She speaks very well English. | She speaks **English very well**. | Don't put an adverb between the verb and its object. |
| He is late always. | He is **always** late. | Frequency adverbs go after *be*. |
| We went last week to Hue. | We went **to Hue last week**. | Place before time. |
| I bought a shirt blue. | I bought a **blue shirt**. | Adjectives go before the noun. |
:::

## In context

> Last Sunday, my wife and I **went to a small market in Hoi An**. We **often go** there **at weekends**. My wife **bought a beautiful long silk dress**, and I **found a small round wooden box** for our daughter. The seller **spoke English very well**. We **had lunch at a little café by the river** and **got home at about six**.

::: details Vietnamese translation
Chủ nhật tuần trước, vợ chồng tôi đi một khu chợ nhỏ ở Hội An. Cuối tuần chúng tôi thường đến đó. Vợ tôi mua một chiếc váy lụa dài rất đẹp, còn tôi tìm được một chiếc hộp gỗ tròn nhỏ cho con gái. Người bán hàng nói tiếng Anh rất giỏi. Chúng tôi ăn trưa ở một quán cà phê nhỏ bên sông và về đến nhà lúc khoảng 6 giờ.
:::

## Remember

::: warning SUMMARY
- General order: **S + V + O + manner + place + time**.
- Adjectives go **before** the noun, in the order **opinion – size – age – shape – colour – origin – material – purpose**.
- Frequency adverbs go **before a main verb** and **after be**; don't put an adverb between the verb and its object.
- **Place before time.**
:::
